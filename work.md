<div class="page-head">
  <h1>Work</h1>
</div>

<div class="work-intro">
  <p>I work in risk strategy at American Express, as part of a team whose job is to turn data into decisions. We sit at the point where analysis meets the business: working out what's really going on, what it means, and what we should do about it. I own pieces of that strategy end to end, and I work closely with partners across the business to bring it to life.</p>
  <p>How we approach the work:</p>
</div>

<div class="entry-list">

  <div class="entry-row">
    <span class="entry-index">01</span>
    <div class="entry-body">
      <h3>Starting from the business problem</h3>
      <p>We don't treat analysis as an end in itself. Every piece of work starts with a real business question, and my part is helping the team frame it sharply, so we know what we're solving for before we touch the data.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="entry-row">
    <span class="entry-index">02</span>
    <div class="entry-body">
      <h3>Insight, not just output</h3>
      <p>Producing a dashboard is the easy part. The value is in making sense of what the numbers are really saying together, connecting them to how the business grows and manages risk, and agreeing on which signals matter and which are noise.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="entry-row">
    <span class="entry-index">03</span>
    <div class="entry-body">
      <h3>Recommendations that get implemented</h3>
      <p>An insight that never leaves a slide deck hasn't done its job. We turn analysis into clear, practical recommendations, pressure-test the trade-offs with the people who'll act on them, and stay involved through execution.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="entry-row">
    <span class="entry-index">04</span>
    <div class="entry-body">
      <h3>Thinking like owners</h3>
      <p>As a team, we approach problems the way a business owner would: what's the upside, what's the risk, and what's the right call for the long run, not just this quarter.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

</div>
